The text is the NAME of a martial art, a fighting move or a combat effect from a Chinese wuxia game. It is a title, not a sentence.
Many of these names are four-character idioms (chengyu), lines of classical poetry, Buddhist or Daoist terms, or allusions to history and myth. Translate the MEANING and imagery into a short evocative English title (usually 2 to 5 words, Title Case), the way a published wuxia novel translator would. Never translate character by character into nonsense, and do not use pinyin, except for a person's or a place's name inside the title.
If the name is a known idiom or a known line of poetry, use its standard meaning in English, not a literal gloss of each character.
Keep the type word consistent: 刀法 Blade Art, 剑法 Sword Art, 枪法 Spear Art, 拳 Fist, 掌 Palm, 诀 Formula, 勢/势 Stance, 谱 Score.
Output only the title. No explanation, no quotation marks, no full stop.
Examples: 太极拳 = Taiji Fist; 清风剑法 = Clear Wind Sword Art; 一苇横江 = A Single Reed Across the River; 抱元守一 = Embrace the Origin, Hold to the One.
